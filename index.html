Brief Bio

I am a Ph.D. graduate in Computer Science and Engineering from the University of Nebraska-Lincoln, advised by Prof. Nirnimesh Ghose (August 2025). My academic journey spans three continents: I received my M.S. in Computer Applied Technology from Changchun University of Science and Technology, China (2020), and my B.Eng in Electronic and Computer Engineering from Nnamdi Azikiwe University, Nigeria (2016).

Research Interests

My research secures the critical infrastructure of emerging wireless networks. I specialize in message integrity and authentication across diverse and challenging environments, including:

  • Agricultural IoT (Underground sensor networks)
  • Autonomous Vehicles (V2X security)
  • Molecular Communication (Nano-scale security)

I combine machine learning with cryptographic protocols to build resilient systems that can withstand sophisticated adversarial attacks.

Research Projects

Agricultural IoT Security

Tackling the essential issue of building trust within agricultural IoT network applications. Strategies integrate physical layer features with cryptographic primitives. Funded by NSF under grants CNS-2331191, CNS-2225161, CNS-1619285, ECCS-2030272.

Autonomous Vehicle Security

Developing methods to prevent adversaries from seizing control of self-governing devices. We use trajectory and motion vector data to ensure message integrity and authentication. Funded by Nebraska Center for Energy Sciences Research (NCESR).

Radio Frequency Fingerprinting (On-going)

Research on fingerprinting in underground networks for message integrity verification using machine learning methods. Funded by NSF.

Molecular Communication Security (On-going)

Investigating lightweight approaches to secure communication between nanodevices in biological environments to prevent bootstrapping attacks. Funded by NSF.
