Ebuka Philip Oguchi

Assistant Professor · Cybersecurity & Wireless Systems

I develop authentication, message-integrity verification, and trust-establishment mechanisms for emerging wireless and cyber-physical systems. My research combines wireless measurements, physical-layer security, signal processing, machine learning, and lightweight protocols to protect communication in resource-constrained environments.

I am an Assistant Professor of Computer and Information Sciences at Spelman College. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Nebraska–Lincoln in 2025.

002 Research

My research develops authentication, message-integrity verification, and trust-establishment mechanisms for emerging wireless and cyber-physical systems, with particular attention to resource-constrained and adversarial environments.

Domain: agri-iot

Underground sensor network security

Domain: v2x

Autonomous & vehicular network credential verification

Domain: nano-comm

Molecular / nano-scale communication security

Domain: loc-auth

Physical-layer & location verification

Domain: applied-ml

Applied machine learning for adversarial environments

Domain: cs-ed

Broadening participation in computing — inclusive curriculum design

Research Methods & Platforms

Wireless testbeds and software-defined radio · signal and channel analysis · machine learning · embedded prototyping · formal security verification · Python, MATLAB, C/C++, USRP, GNU Radio, and ProVerif

Active Projects

Research theme

Agricultural IoT Security

Goal — Establishing trust in underground sensor networks where physical access is difficult.
Method — Physical-layer channel features combined with lightweight cryptographic primitives, without draining sensor batteries.

NCESR-supported work

Autonomous Vehicle Security

Goal — Detecting forged or inconsistent messages in connected and autonomous vehicle networks.
Method — Trajectory and motion-vector information helps determine whether transmitted messages are consistent with observed vehicle behavior.

Ongoing study

Wireless Location & Channel Fingerprinting

Goal — Authenticating wireless devices and transmissions using environment-dependent signal characteristics.
Method — Signal processing and machine learning over channel measurements collected in underground and over-the-air environments.

Ongoing study

Molecular Communication Security

Goal — Securing nano-scale communication in biological environments.
Method — Lightweight protocols preventing bootstrapping attacks between nanodevices where traditional cryptography is too heavy.

Submitted manuscript

LocaRadio: RF-Fingerprint Location Authentication

Goal — Verifying device location as an authentication factor for wireless networks.
Method — RF-fingerprint-based location authentication, submitted to IEEE ICNC.
