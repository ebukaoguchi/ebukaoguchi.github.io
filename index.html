Profile

Research grounded in secure communication.

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer and Information Sciences at Spelman College. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the School of Computing at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln in 2025, where I was advised by Professor Nirnimesh Ghose.

My academic journey spans three continents. I earned an M.S. in Computer Applied Technology from Changchun University of Science and Technology in China and a B.Eng. in Electronic and Computer Engineering from Nnamdi Azikiwe University in Nigeria.

Across agricultural IoT, connected vehicles, molecular communication, and other resource-constrained environments, I study how physical measurements and lightweight protocols can strengthen the authenticity and integrity of exchanged information.

Securing systems where conventional assumptions break down.

My research is organized around four connected themes, each focused on trustworthy communication in challenging environments.

My research develops authentication, message-integrity verification, and trust-establishment mechanisms for emerging wireless and cyber-physical systems, with particular attention to resource-constrained and adversarial environments.

Active Projects

01

Wireless Authentication for Agricultural IoT

Challenge
Underground and resource-constrained sensors operate where physical access, energy, and conventional location services are limited.
Approach
Combine propagation and channel characteristics with lightweight security mechanisms to establish trust and verify messages.
Impact
Support more resilient sensing and data exchange in agricultural IoT deployments.
02

Connected and Autonomous Vehicle Security

Challenge
Safety messages can be forged or made inconsistent with a vehicle’s observable physical behavior.
Approach
Use trajectory and motion-vector information to evaluate whether transmitted claims match observed vehicle behavior.
Impact
Enable additional evidence for credential and message verification in vehicular networks.
03

Wireless Location and Channel Fingerprinting

Challenge
Device identity alone may not establish that a transmission came from an expected location or environment.
Approach
Apply signal processing and machine learning to time-domain and channel measurements collected across wireless environments.
Impact
Provide environment-dependent evidence for authenticating devices and transmissions.
04

Security for Molecular Communication

Challenge
Nanoscale communication systems have severe computational and energy limits that constrain conventional security techniques.
Approach
Study threat models and lightweight trust-establishment mechanisms suited to molecular and nanocommunication systems.
Impact
Help establish a clearer security foundation for emerging communication in biological environments.
Research theme

Agricultural IoT Security

Goal — Establishing trust in underground sensor networks where physical access is difficult.
Method — Physical-layer channel features combined with lightweight cryptographic primitives, without draining sensor batteries.

NCESR-supported work

Autonomous Vehicle Security

Goal — Detecting forged or inconsistent messages in connected and autonomous vehicle networks.
Method — Trajectory and motion-vector information helps determine whether transmitted messages are consistent with observed vehicle behavior.

Ongoing study

Wireless Location & Channel Fingerprinting

Goal — Authenticating wireless devices and transmissions using environment-dependent signal characteristics.
Method — Signal processing and machine learning over channel measurements collected in underground and over-the-air environments.

Ongoing study

Molecular Communication Security

Goal — Securing nano-scale communication in biological environments.
Method — Lightweight protocols preventing bootstrapping attacks between nanodevices where traditional cryptography is too heavy.

Submitted manuscript

LocaRadio: RF-Fingerprint Location Authentication

Goal — Verifying device location as an authentication factor for wireless networks.
Method — RF-fingerprint-based location authentication, submitted to IEEE ICNC.
